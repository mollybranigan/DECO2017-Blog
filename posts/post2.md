# Note
After identifying the general requirements of the project, I started thinking about what kind of community I actually want to design for. I found it helpful to come up with a few different ideas rather than committing to the first one.
One idea I considered was a student-focused community hub, where university students could share study resources, advice, and experiences. This seemed useful because it is something I can personally relate to, and it would have clear types of content such as notes, tips, and questions. However, I realized that this space is already quite saturated with platforms like discussion boards and group chats. It also raised questions about moderation and content quality, which could make it harder to manage.
Another idea was a local events and activities hub. Users could share events happening in their area, such as markets, fitness classes, or social meetups. This idea aligns well with the “community” aspect of the brief and encourages real-world interaction. However, it introduces some complexity around location data and keeping information up to date. There is also a question of whether users would consistently contribute enough content to keep the platform active.
I also briefly considered a fitness-related community, which later developed into the running discussion idea I explore in the next post. Fitness communities tend to have strong engagement, but they also often rely on tracking and performance data, which might be difficult to implement with the given tech stack.
When comparing these ideas, I started thinking more critically about feasibility. It is not just about what sounds interesting, but what I can realistically design and justify within the scope of the assignment. For example, anything that depends heavily on real-time updates or external integrations might not be practical.
I also began prioritizing simplicity. A smaller, well-defined feature set that clearly meets user needs is likely stronger than a more complex idea that I cannot fully justify or implement.
At this stage, I have not fully committed to one final concept, but I am narrowing down my options by considering how clear the user need is, how complex the implementation would be, and whether the idea offers something distinct.
