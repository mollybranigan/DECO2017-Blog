# Note
An idea that I had but did not end up using was a discussion forum for runners. I enjoy running and racing, from 5k to half-marathons, so I thought I could provide some real insight into this project. Having an online community to help with training plans and injuries is something I would personally find useful. In addition, having different sub-discussions based on where people live and the distances they enjoy running could be helpful. Being able to share running routes, upcoming races, or post about local run clubs seemed like something runners would actually engage with.
At first, this idea felt strong because it had a clear audience and purpose. Runners often look for advice, motivation, and community, especially when training for events. It also naturally supports interaction through comments and shared experiences, which aligns well with the core requirements I identified earlier.
However, there were two key issues that led me to not go further with this running discussion page.
The first is a safety issue. While runners love to share routes and run clubs, having individuals openly discussing when and where they are running felt like it could lead to serious risks. I have heard stories about runners posting their routes online and unintentionally revealing patterns about where they live or when they are alone. This creates potential for stalking or other harmful situations. Even if the platform included disclaimers or privacy settings, the risk still exists. From a design perspective, this made me question whether I would need to build additional safety features, which would increase complexity and move away from the scope of the assignment.
My second issue with the running idea is that Strava already exists. While it is not exactly a discussion forum, it already provides a strong social platform for runners and other athletes. Users can post their runs, follow others, join groups, and interact through likes and comments. Once I realized this, my idea started to feel less distinct. It would essentially be a simplified or less functional version of something that already works well.
While my version focused more on discussion rather than performance tracking, I was not convinced that this difference was strong enough to attract users. People are unlikely to switch platforms or use an additional one unless it offers something clearly unique or better. This made me rethink whether the idea had enough value to justify building it.
Overall, this idea helped me better understand the importance of not just identifying a user need, but also considering safety, competition, and feasibility. Even though I did not pursue it, it was useful in shaping how I evaluate future ideas.
