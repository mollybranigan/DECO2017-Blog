# Note
When I first read the BlaBla Corp brief, what stood out most was the idea of a “community hub.” It is not just a generic social media platform, but something more focused. The goal is to bring together people with a shared interest and make it easy for them to connect and share information. That immediately made me think that the success of the app depends less on having lots of features and more on choosing the best features.
One of the first functional requirements I identified is that users need to be able to share content. Without this, there is no real community. However, “sharing content” can mean a lot of things. It could be posts, comments, images, or even events. At this stage, I am trying not to assume too much and instead think about what is actually necessary versus what is optional. At a minimum users need to be able to share text posts.
Another core requirement is interaction. If users can post but no one can respond, the platform would feel static. So commenting, liking, or some form of feedback seems essential. However, I am already aware that adding too many interaction features could complicate the design and make the app harder to build within the given tech stack.
The brief also mentions that users already have a BlaBla Corp login, which removes the need for authentication in this project. This simplifies things a lot and lets me focus more on the actual community features rather than account management.
I also started thinking about constraints early on. The required tech stack (MojoJS, SQLite, HTMX) suggests that the app should stay relatively simple. This means I probably should not design something that depends on real-time updates or very complex data relationships, at least not in the initial version.
Another important requirement is accessibility. Designing accessibility standards means I need to think about things like contrast, navigation, and screen reader compatibility from the beginning, not as an afterthought. This may influence design decisions later, especially around layout and interaction patterns.
At this point, I am not locked into a specific idea yet. Instead, I am focusing on understanding what any successful community hub would need: content, interaction, and a clear sense of purpose. My next step is to explore different possible communities and evaluate which one makes the most sense based on feasibility and user value.
